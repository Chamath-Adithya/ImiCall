# An open project with a sustainable service

ImiCall’s commercial direction is to charge for convenience, infrastructure and support—not for selling personal data. This document is a plan, not an offer, live product, price list or service-level agreement.

## Revenue paths

| Offering | What a customer would pay for | Work required before offering it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Managed hosting | Operating the official HTTPS/signaling service and relay infrastructure | Reliable TURN, cost controls, abuse handling, monitoring and clear service terms |
| Deployment and support | Installing and maintaining an organization’s own instance | Reproducible deployment guides, defined support scope and capacity |
| Enterprise maintenance | Reviewed upgrades, integration help and incident support | A contract with defined limits, availability and responsibilities |
| Sponsorship | Supporting ongoing work voluntarily | An actual funding account and transparent expectations |

No payment collection, paid plan, funding account or enterprise SLA is implemented merely by adding this file. Do not publish invented pricing or a checkout that does not exist.

## Open-source rights and commercial use

An open-source license permits commercial use by other people too. Copyright ownership and the ability to charge do not make the maintainer the only person allowed to run a competing service. An AGPL release can require source availability for covered modified network software under its terms; it does not prohibit competitors.

The proposed model is AGPL-3.0-only with same-license DCO contributions and separately managed branding. The actual LICENSE file, once approved and published, controls; this plan alone does not grant a license.

The maintainer can offer different terms only for material over which he has the necessary rights. A DCO is not a contributor license agreement and does not permit automatic proprietary relicensing of others’ contributions. If closed-source dual licensing becomes a business requirement, obtain explicit appropriate rights before accepting affected code. Have bespoke contracts and contributor agreements reviewed for the relevant jurisdiction.

## Privacy commitments

Do not fund operations by selling contacts, recordings, invitation secrets or call metadata. If a paid hosting service needs billing details, keep that system separate from the contact/invitation store, disclose its actual retention and collect only what is needed. Do not quietly add accounts or tracking to the existing account-free flow.

Infrastructure necessarily has costs and may process metadata. Paid service claims must describe the deployed reality, not just repository intentions.
